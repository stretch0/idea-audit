# Reading

Ordered by what they fix, not by fame.

## Demand and customers

**The Mom Test** — Rob Fitzpatrick. About 130 pages, and the highest-value few
hours available to anyone who has not yet spoken to a customer. Teaches you to
ask about their life rather than your idea, so they cannot lie to you politely.

**Deploy Empathy** — Michele Hansen. Interview technique in more depth.

**Demand-Side Sales 101** — Bob Moesta. Jobs-to-be-done applied rather than
theorised. Trains you to hear the job when someone says it out loud.

**Competing Against Luck** — Christensen and Moesta. The theoretical version.

## Solo and bootstrapped

**The Embedded Entrepreneur** — Arvid Kahl. Audience before product. The right
book if distribution is your binding constraint, which it usually is.

**Start Small, Stay Small** — Rob Walling. Written for developers going solo,
and still the best thing on picking a market you can actually reach.

**Company of One** — Paul Jarvis. Staying deliberately small as a strategy
rather than a consolation.

## Strategy

**Good Strategy Bad Strategy** — Richard Rumelt. The difference between a
strategy and a slogan. The bad-strategy examples are worth it alone.

**7 Powers** — Hamilton Helmer. Short, dense, and much more useful than Five
Forces for deciding whether a position is defensible.

## Short and for morale

**Anything You Want** — Derek Sivers. An hour. Honest about which parts were
luck.

**Rework** — Fried and Hansson. Permission to build something small.

---

One caveat: reading is a comfortable way to defer testing. The Mom Test is
much better with a real conversation to apply it to — do both in the same week.
